## Overview

A desktop vacuum former. A plastic sheet is heated in a frame, then lowered onto a perforated bed on which a mould is placed (an object, a 3D-printed form, and so on): suction under the bed pulls the softened sheet against the mould to reproduce its shape.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Forming area | 200 x 200 mm |
| Forming depth | 130 mm |
| Temperature range (heating element) | 160 to 340 °C |
| Compatible sheet thickness | About 0.25 to 1.5 mm |
| Dimensions | 466 x 274 x 315 mm |
| Weight | 13 kg |
| Suction | External vacuum cleaner required (universal adapter), no built-in vacuum pump |

## Capabilities and limitations

### a. Capabilities

- Quickly making moulds or shells from an existing object or a 3D-printed part.
- Prototyping packaging, shells (cosplay, protective covers) or small parts in relief.
- A mould base for casting (plaster, resin, silicone) from the shape obtained.

### b. Limitations

- Forming on one side only: no fine undercuts, and the mould must have enough draft angle to release.
- Forming area and depth limited to 200 x 200 x 130 mm.
- Requires a compatible external vacuum cleaner (the machine has no vacuum pump of its own).
- The quality of the result depends on the thickness and type of plastic: sheets that are too thick or too stiff do not form properly.

## Usable materials

PETG, HIPS, ABS, polystyrene, polypropylene, polycarbonate, polyethylene, acrylic (PMMA). In sheet form, about 0.25 to 1.5 mm thick.
