Welcome to the **"Puzzle Bot"** project! You are going to **design** and **build** your own **machine that solves a jigsaw puzzle**, with no ready-made plans, giving free rein to your imagination and your skills.

Inspired by devices created by [**Mark Rober**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sqr-PdVYhY4), [**Stuff Made Here**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WsPHBD5NsS0) or other open-source projects, this one lets you explore varied fields such as **electronics**, **programming** and **digital fabrication**, while handling components such as stepper motors, servos and Arduino boards with CNC Shields.

The project runs over **one semester, for 90 hours**, which gives you time to immerse yourself fully in every stage of creation.

---

## What you will learn and make

- **Computer-Aided Design (CAD)**: You will learn to **imagine**, **draw** and **model** the parts of your machine with CAD software.

- **Digital fabrication**: Once your parts are designed, you will use the MakerSpace tools to make them. With **3D printing**, **laser cutting** and other rapid prototyping tools and technologies, you have everything you need to **test** and **realise** your ideas.

- **Programming and electronics**: You will program **Arduino** boards and learn to drive **motors** and **servos**. You can also work on **software development** to generate the movements of your machine, or design your own electronic boards.

- **Assembly and calibration**: You will assemble your machine, taking care to **align** and **calibrate** each element to guarantee **optimal** operation. This is where your machine comes to life!

- **Vision**: You will tackle computer vision, which lets your machine locate the pieces and handle them efficiently.

---

## Why this project?

This project follows on from the second-year (I2) project on **drawing machines**. It aims to deepen and structure the skills already used, moving from a first creative exploration to a more complete and more rigorous engineering approach.

You will consolidate your technical choices, improve the reliability and precision of your machines, and add new features tied to real problems (control, automation, interaction with the environment). The project takes place at the [MakerSpace](https://makerspace-amiens.fr), in a collaborative setting, with access to prototyping tools and technical support.

The aim is to move you towards a more professional approach to developing a machine: needs analysis, design, iterations, tests and validation. This project is thus a key step between an exploratory prototype and a finished system.

## Project instructions

### General organisation

The **Puzzle Bot** project represents a total of **75 hours**, organised in several complementary phases.

- **Pre-project phase (25 h)**: This phase is devoted to defining the concept and to the **initial prototyping** of the Puzzle Bot. The aim is to reach a working **Proof of Concept (POC)** that validates the mechanical, electronic and software principles chosen. This stage ends with an **interim assessment** to check the consistency of the project and its technical feasibility.
- **Project phase – finalisation (50 h)**: You continue developing the Puzzle Bot to reach a more accomplished, reliable **final version**. This phase covers improving performance, the robustness of the system, the integration of advanced features (detection, processing, interaction) and **preparing the demonstration** for the **Projects Day**.

In parallel, **15 hours** are devoted to **project management classes**, aimed at structuring your engineering approach: work organisation, planning, progress tracking and technical communication, directly linked to the project.

### Forming teams

- Teams are made up of **6 students** on average, organised in **sub-teams of pairs**.
- To meet the balancing needs of the project, you are invited to **form your pairs** and to **clearly identify the dominant skills** of each pair (mechanics, electronics, embedded software, computer software and so on).
- The **final group** is then built from these pairs, **grouped by skills**, so as to form a coherent, complementary team suited to the technical challenges of the project.

### Method and autonomy

This project is **not a conventional lab** with detailed instructions to follow. You are **in charge of your own success**:

- Use the dedicated site to find leads and resources to get started.
- Take the initiative: ask questions, do research, test different solutions.
- Do not hesitate to **work outside class hours**. The MakerSpace is open almost all week; make the most of it to move your machine forward.

### Expected work and deliverables

#### At the end of the first 25 h (Proof of Concept - POC)

- **Technical presentation - 15 minutes**: Present your technical choices, how your POC works and the challenges you met.
- **OnShape deliverable**: Produce **all the CAD** of your machine in the Onshape working document provided at the start of your project.
- **Technical documentation**: Start writing your technical documentation in the GitHub repository provided at the start of the project. For this first deliverable, it must include at least:
  - The explanation and justification of your **technical choices** (scientific and technical basis).
  - The description of the **involvement and tasks of each member** in the project.
  - The **progress** of the research and of what has been built so far.

#### At the end of the project (final version)

- **OnShape deliverable**: Provide a complete document with the CAD of the finished machine.
- **Technical sources**: Your code, electronic boards and other technical sources gathered in your GitHub repository.
- **Technical documentation**: Document your machine thoroughly, including:
  - The steps to recreate it.
  - Possible improvements.
  - A critical assessment of the technical choices.
  - The quality of your documentation will be assessed on how well it can be reused by others.
- **Final video - 1 minute**: A portrait-format video presenting the finished version of your machine and how it works. This video must be professional in purpose, as if you were presenting your project to a future recruiter. It must clearly show how the machine works and highlight your technical skills, your design approach and your ability to carry an engineering project through to completion.
- **Taking part in the Projects Day**: Prepare your stand to exhibit your machine and the work done.
- **Technical defence - 20 minutes**: An in-depth presentation of your project to a jury.

### Projects Day (June)

The **Projects Day** is a flagship event where you have the chance to exhibit your machine to professionals (teachers from other schools, companies, elected officials and so on). Your work is assessed by a jury on:

- The quality of the machine.
- The drawings produced.
- The clarity and quality of your presentation.

## Tips for success

- **Plan your effort**: Work regularly throughout the semesters to avoid falling behind.
- **Collaborate effectively**: Communicate within your team and share out the tasks well.
- **Be curious**: Explore the resources available, look for inspiration online, and experiment.
- **Make the most of the MakerSpace**: Use the equipment and ask for advice if in doubt.
