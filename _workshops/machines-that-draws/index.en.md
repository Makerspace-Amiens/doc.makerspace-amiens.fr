## Machines That Draws

Welcome to the **"Machines That Draws"** project! You are going to **design** and **build** your own **automated drawing machine**, with no ready-made plans, giving free rein to your imagination and your skills.

Inspired by devices such as the Axidraw and by **open-source** projects, this project lets you explore varied fields such as **electronics**, **programming** and **digital fabrication**, while handling components such as stepper motors, servos and Arduino boards with CNC Shields.

The project runs over **two semesters (30 hours per semester)**, which gives you time to immerse yourself fully in every stage of creation.

## What you will learn and make

- **Computer-Aided Design (CAD)**: You will learn to **imagine**, **draw** and **model** the parts of your machine with CAD software.

- **Digital fabrication**: Once your parts are designed, you will use the MakerSpace tools to make them. With **3D printing**, **laser cutting** and other rapid prototyping tools and technologies, you have everything you need to **test** and **realise** your ideas.

- **Programming and electronics**: You will program **Arduino** boards and learn to drive **motors** and **servos**. You can also work on **software development** to generate the drawings, or design your own electronic boards.

- **Assembly and calibration**: You will assemble your machine, taking care to **align** and **calibrate** each element to guarantee **optimal** operation. This is where your machine comes to life!

## Why this project?

This project lets you develop your technical skills while leaving room for your creativity and spirit of innovation. You will work in a collaborative setting at the [MakerSpace](https://makerspace-amiens.fr), where you have access to tools and support to bring your machine to life.

It is an opportunity to learn by doing and to see your ideas become reality.

So, ready to take up the challenge? Get your ideas and your tools ready… it is up to you to build the **machine that suits you**!

## Project instructions

### General organisation

The **Machines That Draws** project runs over two semesters (2 x 30 hours).

- **Semester 1 (S1)**: You work on the **prototype** of your machine. By the end of this semester, you must have a working **Proof of Concept (POC)**.
- **Semester 2 (S2)**: You improve your machine to develop a more accomplished **final version**. You also work on generating drawings to prepare for the **Projects Day**, where you present your work.

### Forming teams

- Teams must be made up of **4 students**.
- Given the range of skills needed (electronics, mechanics, embedded software and computer software), it is recommended to team up **by complementary skills**, and not only by personal affinity.
- A balanced team, with complementary skills, will be a major asset in meeting the technological challenges.

### Method and autonomy

This project is **not a conventional lab** with detailed instructions to follow. You are **in charge of your own success**:

- Use the dedicated site to find leads and resources to get started.
- Take the initiative: ask questions, do research, test different solutions.
- Do not hesitate to **work outside class hours**. The MakerSpace is open almost all week; make the most of it to move your machine forward.

### Expected work and deliverables

#### At the end of S1 (Proof of Concept - POC)

- **Technical presentation (5 minutes)**: Present your technical choices, how your POC works and the challenges you met.
- **OnShape deliverable**: Share a single document containing all the CAD of your machine.
- **Vertical video (1 minute)**: Make a video in portrait format, intended to present your project professionally, as if you were talking to a future recruiter. Show how your machine works and highlight your skills.

#### At the end of S2 (final version)

- **OnShape deliverable**: Provide a complete document with the CAD of the finished machine.
- **Technical report**: Document your machine thoroughly, including:
  - The steps to recreate it.
  - Possible improvements.
  - A critical assessment of the technical choices.
  - The quality of your report will be assessed on how well it can be reused by others.
- **Final video**: A video presenting the finished version of your machine and how it works.
- **Taking part in the Projects Day**: Prepare your stand to exhibit your machine and the work done.
- **Technical defence**: An in-depth presentation of your project to a jury.

### Projects Day (June)

The **Projects Day** is a flagship event where you have the chance to exhibit your machine to professionals (teachers from other schools, companies, elected officials and so on). Your work is assessed by a jury on:

- The quality of the machine.
- The drawings produced.
- The clarity and quality of your presentation.

## Tips for success

- **Plan your effort**: Work regularly throughout the semesters to avoid falling behind.
- **Collaborate effectively**: Communicate within your team and share out the tasks well.
- **Be curious**: Explore the resources available, look for inspiration online, and experiment.
- **Make the most of the MakerSpace**: Use the equipment and ask for advice if in doubt.
