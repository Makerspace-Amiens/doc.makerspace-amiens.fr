## What is 3D printing?

Adding material, layer after layer

---

## Manufacturing by adding material

<div class="columns" markdown="1">
<div markdown="1">

**Additive manufacturing**

- Material **added** in layers
- Complex and hollow shapes
- Very little waste
- Prototypes, small series

</div>
<div markdown="1">

**Subtractive manufacturing**

- Material **removed** from a block
- Limited tool access
- Chips and offcuts
- Large series, precision

</div>
</div>

At the start, two processes: **FDM** (fused filament) and **SLA** (resin cured by light).
{: .caption}

[What is 3D printing?](/docs/concepts/3d-print-introduction/#what-is-3d-printing){: .doc-link}

<aside class="notes" markdown="1">
Subtractive example: milling, which removes material from a block.
</aside>

## The first processes

1974-2000: from idea to industry

---

## A timeline

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1974 | David E. H. Jones describes the concept in an article |
| 1981 | Hideo Kodama: rapid prototyping with resin |
| 1984 | Charles Hull files the stereolithography patent |
| 1986 | Hull founds 3D Systems: first SLA printer sold |
| 1988 | Carl Deckard patents laser sintering (SLS) |
| 1989 | Scott Crump (Stratasys) patents fused filament (FDM) |
| 1992 | DTM sells the first industrial SLS printer |
{: .table-dense}

Until the 2000s, 3D printing remains an industrial tool for rapid prototyping.
{: .caption}

[History](/docs/concepts/3d-print-introduction/#history){: .doc-link}

## The forgotten pioneers of 3D printing

<p class="big-number">1984</p>

Jean-Claude André, Alain Le Méhauté and Olivier de Witte develop, in France, a process close to stereolithography.

- {: .fragment .fade-up} Their employer, CILAS Alcatel, sees **no commercial potential** in it
- {: .fragment .fade-up} The patent is **abandoned**; Charles Hull files his own a few weeks later

## Patents and open source

Protecting an invention, or sharing it

---

## Two ways of moving a technology forward

| | Patent | Open source |
|---|---|---|
| **Principle** | Temporary monopoly on exploitation | Free sharing of plans and code |
| **Duration** | 20 years, then public domain | Unlimited |
| **Condition** | New, inventive, industrially applicable | A free licence |
| **Effect** | Protects the investment | Speeds up collective innovation |
{: .table-dense}

The two coexist: Prusa Research publishes its plans while protecting some components.
{: .caption}

[Patents and open source](/docs/concepts/3d-print-introduction/#patents--open-source){: .doc-link}

---

## The end of the FDM patents

- {: .fragment .fade-up} **2000**: the first FDM patents expire
- {: .fragment .fade-up} **2005**: launch of the open-source **RepRap** project
- {: .fragment .fade-up} **2009**: the FDM patents expire completely

Anyone can now build FDM printers: prices collapse.
{: .fragment .fade-up}

[Expiry of the patents](/docs/concepts/3d-print-introduction/#expiry-of-the-patents-2000--2009){: .doc-link}

## A political project

> "So the replicating rapid prototyping machine will allow the revolutionary ownership, by the proletariat, of the means of production." Adrian Bowyer

- **Autonomy**: making things without depending on big industry
- **Decentralisation**: redistributing the means of production
- **Sharing**: common tools and knowledge, a local economy

[A political message](/docs/concepts/3d-print-introduction/#a-political-message){: .doc-link}

<aside class="notes" markdown="1">
Bowyer goes on: "without the dangerous aspects of revolution, nor those of industry", and calls this "Darwinian Marxism". RepRap: "a way for humans to bring machines to those who need them".
</aside>

<p class="big-number">÷ 10</p>

## Bambu Lab: the controversies

- **Cloud dependency**: in August 2023, an outage starts prints on its own or locks machines
- **Data security**: files and logs pass through the brand's servers
- **Closed ecosystem**: Bambu Connect forces a proprietary intermediary on third-party software

A consumer printer… but a proprietary one.
{: .caption}

---

## The AGPL affair (2026)

1. A developer publishes an OrcaSlicer fork that bypasses Bambu Connect
2. Bambu Lab threatens him: he withdraws his project
3. The problem: Bambu Studio derives from PrusaSlicer, under the **AGPLv3** licence
4. The SFC identifies two violations of the licence
5. Bambu Lab backs down; the SFC launches **Baltobu** to free the ecosystem

Free software also defends itself collectively.
{: .caption}

## Five stages, applied to 3D printing

| Stage | 3D printing |
|---|---|
| 1. Trigger | 1980s: SLA and FDM |
| 2. Peak of expectations | 2010s: "print everything at home" |
| 3. Trough of disillusionment | Mid-2010s: cost, slowness, limited materials |
| 4. Slope of enlightenment | 2020: concrete and industrial uses |
| 5. Plateau of productivity | Today: prototyping, medicine, industry |

A useful model for any emerging technology: beware of the promises at the top of the curve.

## Key takeaways
